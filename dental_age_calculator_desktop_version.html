How to use. Stage each of the seven mandibular teeth by clicking the cell that matches what you see on the panoramic radiograph. The calculator will automatically compute five dental age estimates: the classical Demirjian 1973 (7 teeth), the Willems 2001 Belgian adaptation (7 teeth), the Thai Duangto 2016 (7 teeth), and the two Thai Duangto 2018 four-teeth methods. Methods that need teeth you have not yet staged will show a hint. Scores are sex-specific because girls reach the same dental developmental state at younger chronological ages than boys.

About this tool. Developed by Chanika Manmontri with the assistance of Claude (Anthropic), 2026. Intended for teaching and research — not for clinical or forensic decision-making without verification against original source materials. Chronological age = (date of X-ray − date of birth) / 365.25. Last updated: 26 August 2026.

Valid age ranges and reference samples.

  • Demirjian 1973 — French-Canadian reference sample of 1,446 boys and 1,482 girls aged 2–20 yr. Useful range 3.0–16.0 yr (bounds of the published conversion table).
  • Demirjian & Goldstein 1976 (4-teeth scoring, used by Duangto 2018) — extended French-Canadian sample of 2,407 boys and 2,349 girls; percentile standards published for 2.5–17.0 yr.
  • Willems 2001 — Belgian Caucasian sample; 1,029 boys (age range 1.8–18.0 yr) and 1,087 girls (2.1–18.0 yr) were used to build the scoring tables; validation sample of 195 boys (2.3–17.4 yr) and 160 girls (2.4–18.0 yr). Effective working range ~3–17 yr.
  • Duangto 2016 (7 teeth) — Thai sample; 487 males and 647 females aged 6–15 yr.
  • Duangto 2018 (4 teeth, Methods I and II) — Thai sample; 360 males and 360 females aged 7–15 yr.

Results whose chronological age falls outside these ranges are flagged in the result note.

95% predictive intervals are computed as ±1.96 × SEP, where SEPs are taken from the original papers. Willems 2001 does not report a formal SEP; the standard deviations of the error in the validation sample (0.9 yr for boys, 1.3 yr for girls) are used here as an approximation. Duangto 2016 reports 0.83 yr (M) / 0.90 yr (F) for the seven-teeth Thai equation and 0.87 yr (M) / 0.73 yr (F) for the classical Demirjian method when applied to the Thai validation sample. Duangto 2018 reports 0.818 / 0.766 yr for Method I and 0.843 / 0.790 yr for Method II.
